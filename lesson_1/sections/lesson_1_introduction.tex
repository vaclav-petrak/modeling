% --- JOURNAL CLUB INTRO ---
\begin{frame}{Journal Club is a discussion of research papers}
   A journal club is a group that meets to critically evaluate articles in academic literature. Journal clubs:
     \begin{itemize}
        \item Encourage critical thinking.
        \item Help in understanding the studied concepts and methods.
        \item Help keep up with the latest research.
        \item Improve communication and presentation skills.
        \item Simulate the experience of a scientist.
    \end{itemize}
    \vspace{1em}
    \textbf{The main purpose in this course is to explore how the methods we learned are applied in current research.}
\end{frame}

% --- JC CONTENT ---
\begin{frame}{The Journal Club presentation will cover:}
    \begin{description}
    \small
        \setlength{\itemsep}{1em}

        \item[Introduction:] Provide a high-level overview of the background of the research topic and the main objectives of the paper.
        \item[Methods:] Give an overview of the methodology used in the paper.
        \item[Results:] Highlight the key results and conclusions.
        \item[Discussion:] Discuss the practical implications of the research, its applications in real-world situations, and any limitations.
        \item[Conclusions:] Summarize the main contributions and overall findings of \textbf{the paper}. Reiterate its key takeaways.
        \item[Q\&A:] Answer questions from the audience (lecturer).
    \end{description}
\end{frame}

\subsection{Introduction to Modeling}

% --- MODELING INTRO ---
\begin{frame}[t]{A model is a representation of a \textbf{system}}
    \small
    \begin{columns}
        \begin{column}{0.65\textwidth}
            \begin{itemize}
                \item A model is a representation of a \textbf{system} that enables us to investigate its properties and \textbf{predict} future outcomes.
                \begin{itemize}
                    \item A \textbf{system} is a set of components that interact with each other within a boundary to function as a whole (e.g., Solar system, rabbit and fox populations).
                \end{itemize}
                \item In modeling, we aim to identify components, relationships, and behaviors to predict system dynamics.
                \only<2->{
                    \item Modeling always requires simplification (abstraction).
                }
                \only<3>{
                    \item \textbf{A mathematical model uses mathematical equations to describe a system.}
                }
            \end{itemize}
        \end{column}
        
        \begin{column}{0.35\textwidth}
            \color{primary}
            
            \only<1>{
                \begin{center}
                   \includegraphics[scale = 0.15]{images/dummy.png}  
                \end{center}
            }
            
            \only<2>{
                Example of a model: \\
                SIR (susceptible, infectious, and recovered) model for dynamics of infectious diseases
                \vspace{1em}
                
                \includegraphics[scale = 0.12]{images/sir.png}

\end{frame}
