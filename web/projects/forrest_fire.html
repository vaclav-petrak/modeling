Forest Fire Simulation

Modeling fire spread dynamics using cellular automata and environmental parameters.

🌲 Focus: Cellular Automata & Environmental Modeling

Introduction

Figure 1: Example of a cellular automaton modeling forest fire progression.

Forest fires exhibit complex behavior arising from simple interactions between fire and vegetation. Cellular automata provide an effective way to model these dynamics, allowing us to understand fire spread patterns.

In this project, you will implement a forest fire simulation that captures the essential features of fire propagation while considering environmental factors.

Task

  1. Core Implementation: Implement the basic forest fire cellular automaton: a 2D grid where each cell can be in one of three states: empty, containing a tree, or burning. At each time step, an empty cell may grow a tree with a probability, while a tree cell will catch fire if any of its neighboring cells is burning or may spontaneously ignite with a small probability.
  2. Advanced Features: Incorporate at least two more of the following features:
    • Probabilistic Fire Spread: Replace deterministic rules of fire spread: a fire has a chance of either staying on fire or going out, and the trees around a fire have a chance of igniting or not.
    • Wind Dynamics: Add directional wind effects that modify fire spread probabilities based on wind speed and direction.
    • Terrain Integration: Include different terrain types (such as flat land, water bodies, roads, elevation) that affect fire spread patterns.
    • Vegetation Diversity: Model multiple vegetation types with different flammability characteristics and burn rates.
    • Weather Systems: Implement changing weather conditions that affect fire behavior (humidity, temperature, precipitation).
    • Custom Feature: Another feature of similar complexity of your choice.

Discussion Points

In your final report, be sure to address the following:

  • Core Mechanics: Explain your implementation of the core fire spread mechanics, including state transitions and propagation rules.
  • Visualizations: Demonstrate your simulation results using visualizations showing fire progression over time.
  • Advanced Features: Present the two additional features you chose to implement, discussing your approach and any challenges you encountered.
  • Emergent Patterns: Show examples of emergent patterns in your simulation, such as fire fronts, spotting, or barrier effects.
  • Parameter Analysis: Analyze how different parameters (vegetation density, wind conditions, terrain features) affect fire spread.
  • Model Comparison: Compare your simulation's behavior to documented forest fire patterns and discuss model limitations.
  • Literature Review: Compare your implementation to current research in forest fire modeling.
  • Reflection: Share what you learned about cellular automata and their application to environmental modeling through this project.
