Predator-Prey Agent-Based Simulation

Exploring population dynamics through individual agent behaviors and interactions.

🐺 Focus: Agent-Based Modeling & Ecosystem Dynamics

Introduction

Figure 1: Example of an agent-based predator-prey simulation environment.

Predator-prey dynamics have traditionally been studied using Lotka-Volterra equations, which describe population changes through differential equations. However, agent-based models provide an alternative approach by simulating individual agents with specific behaviors and interactions.

In this project, you will develop an agent-based simulation to explore predator-prey interactions, focusing on how individual behaviors influence overall population dynamics.

Task

Implement a basic agent-based predator-prey model and incorporate at least two additional features from the list below:

  • Multi-Level Interactions: Introduce a food chain involving more than two agent types (e.g., predators hunt prey, prey consume vegetation).
  • Terrain Factors: Implement different terrain types that affect movement, survival chances, or interactions (e.g., water bodies as barriers, forests as hiding spots).
  • Seasonal Changes: Simulate seasonal variations that influence predator-prey interactions, such as food availability.
  • Energy Consumption and Replenishment: Assign energy levels to agents that deplete over time, requiring food intake to sustain survival and reproduction.
  • Consumption Limits: Impose constraints on food consumption, such as a maximum intake per agent or competition for resources.
  • Custom Feature: Another feature of similar complexity of your choice.

Discussion Points

In your final report, be sure to address the following:

  • Implementation Details: Explain your implementation of the core predator-prey model, detailing agent behaviors, movement rules, and interaction mechanics.
  • Results Demonstration: Demonstrate your results using visualizations, such as animations, population trend graphs, or heatmaps of agent distribution.
  • Feature Analysis: Present the two additional features you chose to implement, discussing their impact on system dynamics and any challenges you faced.
  • Emergent Behaviors: Analyze emergent behaviors in your simulation, such as population oscillations, predator extinction, or stable coexistence.
  • Initial Conditions: Explore how different initial conditions (e.g., varying predator-to-prey ratios, resource availability, or mobility constraints) influence outcomes.
  • Model Comparison: Compare your simulation results with the predictions of traditional Lotka-Volterra models, identifying similarities and discrepancies.
  • Methodology Comparison: Discuss the advantages and limitations of agent-based modeling compared to equation-based approaches in studying predator-prey systems.
  • Reflection: Share insights gained about population dynamics, ecosystem interactions, and the role of stochasticity in ecological modeling.
