Nagel-Schreckenberg Traffic Simulation

Exploring traffic dynamics and emergent patterns using cellular automata.

🚗 Focus: Cellular Automata & Traffic Modeling

Introduction

Figure 1: Example of spontaneous jam formation and stop-and-go waves.

The Nagel-Schreckenberg model is a cellular automaton that demonstrates how complex traffic patterns emerge from simple rules. This model captures essential features of real traffic flow, including spontaneous jam formation and stop-and-go waves.

In this project, you will implement this model to explore traffic dynamics and understand how individual driver behaviors affect overall traffic patterns.

Task

  1. Core Implementation: You should implement the core Nagel-Schreckenberg rules—acceleration, slowing down, randomization, and vehicle movement on a single-lane road.
  2. Additional Features: In addition, implement at least two more of the following features:
    • Multi-lane Dynamics: Add multiple lanes with lane-changing rules based on speed differences and safety gaps.
    • Driver Behavior Variations: Introduce different driver types (aggressive, cautious, normal) with varying randomization parameters and desired speeds.
    • Road Network Features: Implement traffic lights, speed limits, or similar features.
    • Traffic Monitoring: Add measurement points that collect data on flow rates, density, and average speeds at different road sections.
    • Custom Feature: Other feature of similar complexity of your choice.

Discussion Points

In your final report, be sure to address the following:

  • Implementation Details: Explain your implementation of the core model rules, including how you handled vehicle movement, speed updates, and randomization.
  • Visualizations: Demonstrate your results using clear visualizations and plots.
  • Feature Expansion: Present the two additional features you chose to implement, discussing your approach.
  • Emergent Phenomena: Show examples of emergent phenomena in your simulation, such as traffic jam formation and dissolution.
  • Parameter Analysis: Analyze how different parameters (maximum velocity, vehicle density) affect traffic flow patterns.
  • Real-world Comparison: Compare your simulation's behavior to real-world traffic patterns and discuss limitations.
  • Literature Review: Compare your implementation to recent research literature on traffic flow modeling.
  • Reflection: Share what you learned about cellular automata and their application to traffic systems through this project.
