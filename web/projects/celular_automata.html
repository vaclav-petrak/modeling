Epidemic Spread Modeling

Simulating disease propagation using cellular automata and spatial dynamics.

🦠 Focus: Simulation & Spatial Dynamics

Introduction

Figure 1: Example of an epidemic spread simulation on a cellular grid.

Epidemic spread models play a crucial role in understanding how infectious diseases propagate through populations. Cellular automata provide a grid-based approach to simulating disease spread, enabling explicit representation of both spatial and temporal dynamics.

In this project, you will develop a cellular automata-based simulation to explore how local interactions, movement restrictions, and different transmission rules influence epidemic dynamics.

Task

You should implement a basic cellular automata model for disease spread and incorporate at least two additional features from the list below:

  • Basic Disease Transmission: Implement a grid where cells represent individuals, following a rule-based infection process (e.g., Susceptible-Infected-Recovered (SIR) model).
  • Heterogeneous Population Dynamics: Introduce different population groups with varying susceptibility, recovery rates, or movement behaviors.
  • Quarantine and Movement Restrictions: Implement measures such as lockdowns, travel bans, or isolation zones to study their effects on disease spread.
  • Environmental Factors: Model spatial variations such as high-risk zones (e.g., crowded areas) or environmental influences on transmission.
  • Adaptive Behavior: Allow individuals to change their movement patterns or interactions based on the infection risk in their surroundings.
  • Custom Feature: Another feature of similar complexity of your choice.

Discussion Points

In your final report, be sure to address the following:

  • Implementation Details: Explain your implementation of the core disease transmission model, including how you structured the grid, defined the rules, and managed state transitions.
  • Visualizations: Demonstrate your results using visualizations, such as animations, heatmaps, or step-by-step snapshots.
  • Additional Features: Present the two additional features you chose to implement, discussing your approach and any challenges you encountered.
  • Emergent Behaviors: Show examples of emergent behaviors, such as clustering effects, wave-like transmission, or unexpected epidemic patterns.
  • Initial Conditions: Analyze how different initial conditions (e.g., varying infection rates, population density, or movement restrictions) impact disease spread.
  • Real-world Comparison: Compare your simulation results with real-world epidemic data or established epidemiological models.
  • Methodological Comparison: Discuss how cellular automata compare to other mathematical approaches used in epidemiology, such as differential equation models.
  • Reflection: Share insights gained about epidemic modeling, spatial dynamics, and the strengths and limitations of cellular automata in this context.
